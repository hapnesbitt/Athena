# ATHENA — The HipHop Odyssey: Canonical Song Map

*24 books. 24 contrafacts. Each number is a real song the dancers already know —
bent onto the Homeric beat. This document is the creative source of truth for the
writers' room engine (`scribe.py`). Each entry is the seed of a `BOOKS` registry
entry.*

---

## Part I — Adventures of Telemachus (Books 1–4)

*Telemachus's coming-of-age: the household in decline, the suitors eating the palace
hollow, a boy beginning to become his father's son.*

### Book I — "Deal With It"
**Artist:** Ashnikko
**Homeric content:** The suitors overrun the palace. Athena arrives disguised as
Mentes and plants the idea of the journey in Telemachus.
**Why this song:** Telemachus is furious and powerless — the household in decline,
the suitors treating his home as their personal feast hall. "Deal with it" is what
everyone keeps telling him. He's done dealing with it.
**Contrafact angle:** Telemachus's rage at the situation, the slow gathering of
defiance.

---

### Book II — "Fast"
**Artist:** Saweetie
**Homeric content:** Telemachus calls the assembly, announces his intention to find
his father, and begins preparing the ship.
**Why this song:** He's moving now — the desire to act, to get fast toward something
instead of sitting still while the suitors feast.
**Contrafact angle:** The call to action, the first real move.

---

### Book III — "Freak"
**Artist:** Pitbull
**Homeric content:** Telemachus arrives at Pylos, meets Nestor, encounters the wider
heroic world for the first time.
**Why this song:** New experiences, new influences — the boy stepping off the island
and into the big world. Nestor is a freak for his own glory days.
**Contrafact angle:** Telemachus encountering the world beyond Ithaca; the older
generation showing off.

---

### Book IV — "Rude Boy"
**Artist:** Rihanna
**Homeric content:** Telemachus at Sparta with Menelaus and Helen. Growing
confidence. Back in Ithaca, the suitors plot to ambush him on his return.
**Why this song:** Defiance and growing confidence — he's been out in the world now,
he knows who his father is, and he's done being the rude boy they can push around.
**Contrafact angle:** The suitors as antagonists; Telemachus's spine hardening.

---

## Part II — Wanderings of Odysseus (Books 5–8)

*Odysseus released from Calypso, battered by Poseidon, washed up among the
Phaeacians. The wanderer before we hear his own story.*

### Book V — "My Type"
**Artist:** Saweetie
**Homeric content:** Hermes delivers Zeus's order. Calypso must release Odysseus.
She does — bitterly, sovereignly. Odysseus builds the raft and sails.
**Why this song:** Calypso's number — Odysseus is her type because he sacks cities
from the sea and she is a sea-witch: predator meets predator. The release is on her
terms.
**Contrafact angle:** Calypso's boastful, wounded "My Type" song; the morally murky
partnership; the hinge of the whole poem.
**Status:** ✅ Engine-ready (`scribe.py` Book V, fully implemented)

---

### Book VI — "Rain On Me"
**Artists:** Lady Gaga & Ariana Grande
**Homeric content:** Poseidon destroys Odysseus's raft. He swims for days. Athena
and Poseidon in divine conflict over him. He washes up on Scheria.
**Why this song:** Divine conflict as weather — Odysseus getting absolutely hammered
by the gods, rain on me, I'll survive. Two goddesses (Athena/Ino) pulling him
through.
**Contrafact angle:** The storm, the suffering, the defiant survival.

---

### Book VII — "Mic Drop"
**Artist:** Steve Aoki (BTS)
**Homeric content:** Odysseus presented to the Phaeacian court of Alcinous. His
reputation precedes him; the room takes his measure.
**Why this song:** Odysseus's rep landing in the room — the mic drop of a legendary
warrior walking into a new court. Alcinous's court recognizes greatness.
**Contrafact angle:** Introduction of Odysseus's legend; the court's reaction.

---

### Book VIII — "Every Day"
**Artist:** A$AP Rocky
**Homeric content:** The Phaeacian games. Demodocus the bard sings of Troy and
Odysseus weeps. The ongoing weight of the voyage.
**Why this song:** The grinding accumulation of the journey — every day another
trial, every day the war follows him, every day the bard sings his own story back
at him.
**Contrafact angle:** Odysseus anonymous in the court, weeping at his own legend.

---

## Part III — Retrospective Narrative (Books 9–12)

*Odysseus tells his own story to the Phaeacians. The famous adventures — in his
own voice, at the dinner table, performing the epic of himself.*

### Book IX — "Bad Habits"
**Artist:** Ed Sheeran
**Homeric content:** The Cicones, the Lotus Eaters, and the Cyclops Polyphemus.
**Why this song:** The bad habits that keep costing them — raiding the Cicones when
they should have run, the Lotus Eaters nearly losing the crew to comfort, and then
Odysseus's worst habit of all: he can't resist telling Polyphemus his real name.
That hubris costs him Poseidon's wrath for the rest of the poem.
**Contrafact angle:** The compulsive bad decision; the name shouted into the dark.

---

### Book X — "Don't Stop"
**Artist:** Megan Thee Stallion
**Homeric content:** Circe's hall. Hermes gives Odysseus the moly antidote. Circe
transforms the crew into beasts. Odysseus, immune, forces her to restore them.
**Why this song:** Circe's number — she works through men one by one turning them
into animals, don't stop, hungry to get a lion out of Odysseus. Her magic meets the
one man it can't hold and she is overpowered, not seduced.
**Contrafact angle:** Dominant, gleeful, relentless power-flex; the immersive
audience-pull (dancers bring real men up as the crew); the power-flip rescue.
**Status:** ✅ Engine-ready (`scribe.py` Book X, fully implemented)

---

### Book XI — "Get Ugly"
**Artist:** Jason Derulo
**Homeric content:** The Nekuia — Odysseus descends to the Underworld to consult
Tiresias. He sees his mother, the shades of heroes, the horror of the dead.
**Why this song:** The descent into the underworld is the ultimate get ugly —
stripped of everything, in the land of the dead, confronting what the journey has
cost. The dead don't clean up for company.
**Contrafact angle:** Odysseus among the shades; the grotesque beauty of the
underworld; the cost of the voyage made visible.

---

### Book XII — "Outta Your Mind"
**Artist:** Lil Jon
**Homeric content:** The Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, and the sacred cattle of
Helios. The crew, starving and desperate, slaughter the forbidden cattle despite
Odysseus's warning. Zeus destroys the ship. Only Odysseus survives.
**Why this song:** Eating the sacred cattle is the definition of outta your mind —
Tiresias warned them, Circe warned them, Odysseus warned them. They did it anyway.
Out of their minds with hunger, with desperation, with the kind of bad judgment that
gets everyone killed.
**Contrafact angle:** The crew's fatal break; the cattle; the destruction; Odysseus
alone on the wreckage.

---

## Part IV — Main Narrative Resumes (Books 13–20)

*Odysseus returns to Ithaca in disguise. The slow, patient preparation for
reckoning.*

### Book XIII — "WOW"
**Artist:** Post Malone
**Homeric content:** The Phaeacians deliver Odysseus to Ithaca. He wakes alone on
the shore. Athena appears, disguises him as a beggar, and they scheme together.
**Why this song:** The wow of finally being home — and immediately putting on rags.
The greatest warrior of the age disguised as a beggar is its own kind of wow. Post
Malone's "WOW" has that low-key swagger under the surface that fits a king in
disguise perfectly.
**Contrafact angle:** The return; the disguise put on; the hidden greatness.

---

### Book XIV — "Without Me"
**Artist:** Halsey
**Homeric content:** Odysseus, still disguised as a beggar, finds the loyal
swineherd Eumaeus. Eumaeus grieves for his lost master without knowing he's talking
to him. Hospitality and faithfulness in the ruins of the household.
**Why this song:** Eumaeus has been keeping the faith without Odysseus — everything
has been falling apart without me, and here's the one man who never stopped
believing. Halsey's version of "Without Me" has the right ache for that kind of
loyal grief.
**Contrafact angle:** The faithful servant; the disguised master; the grief of
twenty years of absence.

---

### Book XV — "Bounce Back"
**Artist:** Little Mix
**Homeric content:** Telemachus returns to Ithaca, avoids the suitors' ambush, and
goes to Eumaeus's hut — where Athena has disguised Odysseus as a beggar. Telemachus
does not recognize his own father.
**Why this song:** Telemachus has been out in the world and is bouncing back — but
the bounce-back lands him face to face with a beggar who is his father, and he
doesn't know it. The non-recognition scene. Athena's disguise holding even against
a son's eyes.
**Contrafact angle:** The reunion that isn't yet a reunion; the disguise between
father and son.

---

### Book XVI — "Lay"
**Artist:** Jason Derulo
**Homeric content:** Athena lifts the disguise and Odysseus reveals himself to
Telemachus. Father and son reunited, weeping, making their plan.
**Why this song:** The revelation — lay it all down, the disguise, the distance, the
years. The most intimate scene in the poem: just the two of them, finally.
**Contrafact angle:** The recognition; the tears; the plan forming between them.

---

### Book XVII — "Walk It Out"
**Artist:** Usher
**Homeric content:** Odysseus, re-disguised as beggar, walks into his own palace.
Argos the old dog recognizes him and dies. The suitors abuse the beggar.
**Why this song:** Walking it out — back into the palace he's been gone from for
twenty years, in rags, watching everything. And Argos: the dog who waited, who sees
through every disguise, who walks out of life the moment his master comes home.
**Contrafact angle:** The walk back in; the dog; the patient observation of the
enemy.

---

### Book XVIII — "No Tears Left To Cry"
**Artist:** Ariana Grande
**Homeric content:** Odysseus among the suitors as beggar. Penelope appears. The
beggar and the suitors interact. The disguise holds.
**Why this song:** Penelope has been crying for twenty years. She's out of tears.
She appears in the hall and the suitors are dazzled — but she's playing them, as
always. No tears left, just strategy.
**Contrafact angle:** Penelope's exhausted strength; the long game she's been
running.

---

### Book XIX — "Savage Love"
**Artist:** Jason Derulo
**Homeric content:** Penelope interviews the disguised Odysseus. She nearly
recognizes him. The nurse Eurycleia washes his feet and *does* recognize the scar —
Odysseus silences her.
**Why this song:** The savage love of a twenty-year marriage tested to its limit —
two people talking around each other, one hiding, one almost knowing. The scar
scene is the most savage intimacy in the poem.
**Contrafact angle:** The almost-recognition; the scar; the trust and the secret.

---

### Book XX — "Up"
**Artist:** Cardi B
**Homeric content:** The eve of the contest. Omens and portents. Odysseus surveys
the hall and steadies himself. Things are about to go up — or come down.
**Why this song:** Everything is about to blow — the tension at maximum, the day of
reckoning one sleep away. Up, up, things are going up.
**Contrafact angle:** The night before; the coiled readiness; the calm before.

---

## Part V — Judgment of the Suitors (Books 21–24)

*The bow. The slaughter. The recognitions. The restoration.*

### Book XXI — "Black Widow"
**Artist:** Iggy Azalea
**Homeric content:** Penelope brings out Odysseus's bow and announces the contest:
string the bow and shoot through twelve axe-heads. She will marry whoever succeeds.
**Why this song:** Penelope is the black widow — she's been running this trap for
twenty years. The bow contest is her killing move; she's been spinning the web the
whole poem and this is when it closes.
**Contrafact angle:** Penelope's long game revealed; the trap sprung.

---

### Book XXII — "All About That Bass"
**Artist:** Meghan Trainor
**Homeric content:** The suitors fail at the bow. Odysseus strings it. The slaughter
begins.
**Why this song:** It's about to get bass — loud, physical, final. The suitors
couldn't even string the bow; Odysseus strings it like it's nothing and then the
bass drops.
**Contrafact angle:** The contest; the failure of the suitors; the bow strung; the
arrow through the axes.

---

### Book XXIII — "New Rules"
**Artist:** Dua Lipa
**Homeric content:** Penelope tests Odysseus even after the slaughter — she orders
the bed moved, knowing only Odysseus knows it cannot be moved (it's built around a
living olive tree). He passes. Recognition. Reunion.
**Why this song:** Penelope's new rules — she doesn't just accept him back because
he says so. She has rules. The bed test is her rule, her sovereignty, her proof.
**Contrafact angle:** The bed test; Penelope's agency in the recognition; the
reunion on her terms.

---

### Book XXIV — "Work B*tch"
**Artist:** Britney Spears
**Homeric content:** The suitors' shades in Hades. Laertes recognized. The families
of the suitors rebel. Athena intervenes and restores peace. Order restored.
**Why this song:** The restoration of a kingdom takes work — Odysseus has been
working for twenty years to get here, and the work isn't done even after the
slaughter. The families rebel, Athena intervenes, peace is brokered. You want your
kingdom back? Work.
**Contrafact angle:** The aftermath; the restoration; the final reckoning.
