Athena School Edition

Three short scenes from a hip-hop retelling of the Odyssey. Each fits one class period with a small cast, and none needs a recording.

All three scripts, ready to print

The scenes

  • Book V · about 8 minutes · 4 speakersCalypso's OfferA goddess offers a man eternal life. He would rather go home and grow old.
  • Book IX · about 10 minutes · 3 speakers, plus a small crewNobodyOdysseus beats the Cyclops by giving a false name. Then he gives the real one.
  • Book XXIII · about 8 minutes · 5 speakers, one of them smallThe Olive TreeAfter twenty years, a stranger says he is her husband. Penelope has one test.

One class period

  1. 5 minutes: teach the beat.
  2. 10 minutes: read the scene aloud around the circle, with no parts yet.
  3. 20 minutes: small groups pick parts and rehearse one section.
  4. 10 minutes: show the work.
  5. 5 minutes: talk through one prompt.

Times on each scene are estimates from the word count. Time your own read.

Making the beat

Count 1, 2, 3, 4. Step on 1. Clap on 2. Step on 3. Clap on 4. Start slow, with a clap about every second and a half. For more drive, add a quick second step right after 3.

Students can also drum on a desk, beatbox, or bring a beat they made themselves. Ask everyone to speak in their own voice. No one needs an accent or a “rap voice.”

Music and rights

No scene here needs a recording. The full Athena was built around popular songs, but this edition uses none of their titles, hooks or lyrics. Use a beat your students make.

If you want to use a real recording: staging one in a show likely needs dramatic rights from the song’s owners, and a school’s usual music license often doesn’t cover that. Check with your school first. This is general information, not legal advice.

About this edition

Who wrote this

Mostly machines. A person chose the story, the structure and the voice of Athena. A local AI model wrote the first draft of the full edition in 2026. Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, rewrote these scenes for school, kept a few of the draft’s best lines, and checked the story against Butcher and Lang’s 1879 translation of Homer. These scenes have not yet been tried in a classroom.
