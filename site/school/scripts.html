Athena School Edition · Book V of the Odyssey · about 8 minutes

Calypso's Offer

Parts: Calypso, Odysseus, Hermes, The Muse, and anyone keeping the beat.

A rule down the left side means the line goes on the beat. Everything else is spoken. Line numbers are for rehearsal.

Upstage, Calypso walks to and fro before a loom made of air, humming. Far downstage, Odysseus sits with his back to all of it, looking out over the audience. The audience is the sea. He hears nothing until she comes to him.

THE MUSE

Seven years on one island, in the navel of the sea.

All the others who came through Troy are home by now, or dead.

This one sits on the rocks and weeps, and the nymph Calypso, that fair goddess, holds him there.

But grey-eyed Athena has spoken for him on Olympus.

5So Zeus, gatherer of the clouds, has sent his messenger.

HERMES

Beat in: Quick and light. Finger snaps. Hermes sets the speed.

Gold on my feet and the wind at my back,

Skim the wave like a cormorant, stay on track.

Long way. Salt. More salt. Still salt.

No town. No temple. That's Zeus's fault.

10Not one burnt offering for miles and miles.

Zeus said go, so I go, with style.

I'm Hermes, the messenger. That's no boast,

Just stating the news on a lonely coast.

Beat out

He sees the cave. He stops.

Okay.

15Cedar on the fire. I could smell it from the beach.

Four springs running. Violets. A vine around the door.

Even a god could stand here and be glad.

He stands there and is glad.

Okay. Work.

CALYPSO

She does not stop weaving.

Hermes of the golden wand.

20Welcome. And what an honor. You never come here.

Gods are no strangers to each other, so say it.

If I can do it, I will. But sit. Eat first.

She sets out ambrosia and nectar. He eats fast.

HERMES

You asked, so here it is, god to goddess.

Zeus sent me. I did not volunteer.

25Who would fly across all that on purpose?

You have a man here, the unluckiest of all who fought at Troy.

His crew is lost. The wind and the wave brought him to you.

Zeus says: send him home, and quickly.

It is not his fate to die far from his people.

30And no god gets around Zeus. That's all there is to say.

The loom stops. Calypso shudders. Then she stamps, once, twice. It becomes her beat.

CALYPSO

Beat in: Her own stamp, slow and hard. Others join in with claps.

Hard. You gods are hard. And jealous, too.

A god takes a lover? Not a word from you.

But a goddess loves a man in the light of day?

You can't stand it. You take him away.

 

35Dawn chose Orion. Artemis shot him.

Demeter chose Iasion. Zeus's bolt got him.

Now it's my turn, and he sends his boy,

Like I stole this man from the walls of Troy.

 

Who saved him? Me. Zeus threw the bolt

40That split his ship. So whose is the fault?

Alone on a keel, half drowned, half dead:

I pulled him in and I gave him bread.

You call this a prison. I call it a shore

Where a drowned man's breathing. Who did more?

45I loved him and kept him. I gave him my word:

No death, no old age. Best offer he's heard.

Beat out

She picks up the shuttle.

But no god gets around Zeus. Fine.

Let him go, over the sea that gives no harvest.

I have no ships. I have no crew. I won't send him.

50But I'll tell him how to get home safe, and I will hide nothing.

HERMES

Good. Do it. And mind Zeus's temper. He remembers.

He is gone.

THE MUSE

She went down to the shore and found him where he always was,

his eyes never dry, his sweet life running out,

looking across the sea that gives no harvest.

55She had stopped pleasing him long ago. He had no ship, no crew, no way to leave.

CALYPSO

Poor man. Stop crying. Don't let your life drain away on this beach.

I'm sending you home, with all my heart.

Get up. Cut long beams. Build a wide raft and deck it high.

I'll load it with bread and water. I'll put clothes on your back

60and send a good wind behind you.

ODYSSEUS

He gets up slowly. He doesn't trust it.

No. What's the plan here, goddess?

A raft? On that? Good ships with a god's own wind don't like that crossing.

I'm not setting foot on any raft unless you swear to me,

a great oath, that you're planning nothing against me.

Calypso smiles.

CALYPSO

65You rogue. Only you would think of that.

All right. Let Earth be my witness, and wide Heaven above,

and the falling water of the Styx, the greatest oath the gods can swear:

I plan no harm to you.

I'm telling you what I would tell myself, if I were where you are.

70I know what's right, too. My heart is not iron.

Two chairs, facing. He sits in the one Hermes left. They eat. Nobody speaks.

CALYPSO

Beat in: Just her fingers on the arm of the chair. Slow.

Son of Laertes, you really want home?

Today? Right now? Then go. Go alone.

But if you knew what the sea has in store,

You'd stay in this house and you'd ask for no more.

75No death. No old age. Think it through.

I know you miss her. I've watched you, too,

Every day at the edge of the sea.

Is she taller? Is she finer than me?

Look at me, man, and answer me. Say.

80A mortal, next to a goddess? No way.

Beat out

He takes his time.

ODYSSEUS

Goddess. Queen. Don't be angry with me.

I know all that. I know it well.

Penelope is wise, but next to you she is nothing to look at.

She will grow old. She will die. You never will.

A pause.

85And still. Every day, I want to go home.

Every day.

Beat in: His own feet. One step at a time.

If some god wrecks me on the wine-dark sea,

I'll take it. I've got that heart in me.

Waves and war, I've had my share.

90Add this to the pile. Put it there.

Beat out

THE MUSE

So soon as early Dawn shone forth, the rosy-fingered,

she gave him an axe of bronze, two-edged, with a handle of olive wood,

and she led him to the edge of the island, where the tall trees grew.

ODYSSEUS

Beat in: The work. Everyone who isn't speaking chops, on counts one and three.

Twenty trees: alder, poplar, pine.

95Cut them, trim them, make straight the line.

Calypso comes with augers. She sets them down where he can reach them. She goes. She says nothing.

Bore every beam and peg it tight.

Deck it and rail it, stem to stern, right.

Mast. Yard. A rudder to steer.

Calypso comes with cloth for the sails. She sets it down. She goes.

Sails I can sew. I'm nearly clear.

100Four days' work, and she's built to float.

Lever her down to the sea. That's my boat.

Beat out

CALYPSO

On the fifth day. She puts a cloak around him. She hands him a bag and a skin.

Water. Grain. The food you like.

She lifts her hand. A warm wind.

Steer by the Bear. Keep it on your left.

She has more to say. She doesn't say it.

Go.

He raises the sail. He is glad. It's the first time we have seen him glad. He doesn't look back. Calypso watches him a while, then walks to the loom, picks up the gold shuttle and starts to hum.

THE MUSE

105Ten days and seven he sailed, and sleep never fell on his eyes.

On the eighteenth day the shadowy hills of the Phaeacians rose up,

like a shield on the misty sea.

And far off, on his way home from the Ethiopians,

the lord who shakes the earth looked up and saw him.

One stomp, everyone. Stop.

Athena School Edition · Book IX of the Odyssey · about 11 minutes

Nobody

Parts: Odysseus, Polyphemus, The Muse, Crew, Cyclopes, and anyone keeping the beat.

A rule down the left side means the line goes on the beat. Everything else is spoken. Line numbers are for rehearsal.

A bare floor. One chair upstage is the cave door. The Crew sit in a huddle. The audience is the court of King Alcinous.

THE MUSE

Hear now of the man of many devices.

Ten years gone from Troy. Wrecked, washed up, and fed

in the hall of King Alcinous, among people who do not know his name.

They feed a stranger first. Then they ask.

5You in the seats: you are that court.

The king has asked. Listen.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) My name? I'll tell you. Remember that I told you.

I am Odysseus, son of Laertes.

My home is Ithaca, a rough rock low in the sea,

10and to me there is nothing sweeter.

Here is what it cost me to say so.

Beat in: Step, clap, step, clap. Slow and heavy.

Came out of Troy with twelve black ships,

Salt on my teeth and the war on my lips.

Six men a ship on the Cicones' shore.

15Lotus? One bite, and you're home no more.

 

Then the Cyclops' land: no law, no plow,

No court, no council. I'm curious now.

I took twelve men and a goatskin of wine,

Dark, sweet, strong, and the gift was mine:

20Maron gave it, Apollo's priest.

One cup in twenty of water: still a feast.

 

We found his cave, and nobody home.

Cheese in the baskets. Lambs. Alone.

CREW

Captain! Take the cheese! Take the lambs! Let's go!

ODYSSEUS

25I looked at my crew and I told them no.

I wanted the giant. I wanted his gift,

The gift that's owed when you're cast adrift.

Beat out

(to the court) It would have been far better to take the cheese.

THE MUSE

Then he came home, that monstrous thing,

30like a wooded peak of the high hills, standing apart and alone.

He threw down his firewood, and the cave shook.

He drove in his flocks. He lifted a doorstone

that twenty-two wagons could not have moved,

and he set it in the door.

Polyphemus fills the space. The Crew shrink back.

POLYPHEMUS

35RUMBLE. ROAR.

This cave mine. These sheep mine.

He milks. He lights the fire. He sees them.

Strangers. Who are you?

Traders? Or pirates, robbing the salt roads?

ODYSSEUS

We are Greeks, lord, blown off course from Troy.

40Men of Agamemnon. You know that name.

We come to your knees. Give us what a stranger is owed.

Fear the gods. Zeus himself looks after strangers.

POLYPHEMUS

You stupid, little man. Or you come from far.

Cyclopes do not fear Zeus. We are better than Zeus.

45I spare you if I want. Not for Zeus.

Where is your ship? Far? Near? Tell me.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) He thought he could catch me. Me.

(to Polyphemus) What ship? Poseidon broke it on your rocks.

We are all that's left.

Polyphemus says nothing. He reaches into the Crew. Two of them step back into the dark and turn away. That is all we see.

POLYPHEMUS

50CRUNCH. CRUNCH.

Men between teeth. No mercy in hunger.

He lies down among his sheep and sleeps.

ODYSSEUS

I put my hand on my sword. I found the place, under the ribs.

One push. And then? Who moves the stone?

Twenty-two wagons couldn't. Eleven men can't.

55We'd die in the dark, behind a dead giant's door.

So I took my hand away, and we waited for the dawn.

THE MUSE

Early Dawn shone forth, the rosy-fingered.

He milked his flocks, all in order. He ate two more.

Then he rolled away the stone, drove his flocks out,

60and set it back as lightly as you'd put the lid on a quiver.

ODYSSEUS

Beat in: Faster and quieter. The Crew whisper it with him.

By the pen lay his club, green olive wood,

Long as a ship's mast. There it stood.

Cut off six feet. Crew, make it clean.

I shaved the point, burned it hard and mean.

65Hid it in the dung where the sheep had been.

Cast lots: who lifts it? Four men in,

The same four men I would have picked.

I make five. Now here's the trick.

Beat out

THE MUSE

Evening. He came home. He set the stone. He milked. He ate two more.

70That was six.

And Odysseus stood up, with a bowl of ivy wood in his hands.

ODYSSEUS

Cyclops. Here. Drink. You've had your meal of men.

Taste what my ship was carrying.

I brought it as an offering, hoping you'd pity me and send me home.

75But you're out of your mind. Who will ever visit you again?

Polyphemus takes the bowl and drinks. Mime it. The bowl can be empty hands.

POLYPHEMUS

Good. GOOD.

Our vines grow grapes. Zeus rains on them. This is different.

This is nectar.

Again. And your name, little man. Tell me.

80I give you a stranger's gift. You'll be glad.

THE MUSE

Three times he poured. Three times, in his folly, the giant drank it down.

ODYSSEUS

You ask my famous name, Cyclops? I'll tell you.

Then give me the gift you promised.

Nobody. My name is Nobody.

85My mother calls me Nobody. My father calls me Nobody.

So do all my friends.

POLYPHEMUS

(slowly, very pleased) Nobody.

Nobody I eat last. All your friends first.

Nobody last.

90That is your gift.

He falls back. He sleeps.

ODYSSEUS

Beat in: One finger on a table. Then two. Then the Crew.

Shove that stake down deep in the ash,

Green wood glowing. Now move. Fast.

Five of us lift it, a god in our chests.

I lean from above and the crew does the rest.

95Turn it like a drill through a ship's thick beam.

Hear it hiss. Now hear him scream.

Beat out

All of this is mime. The stake is made of air. No one touches Polyphemus.

POLYPHEMUS

AAAAH!

CYCLOPES! NEIGHBORS! HELP ME!

CYCLOPES

(from far off, half asleep) Polyphemus! It's the middle of the night!

100What is wrong with you?

Is somebody stealing your sheep? Is somebody killing you?

POLYPHEMUS

FRIENDS! NOBODY IS KILLING ME!

NOBODY! BY A TRICK!

CYCLOPES

Nobody? Nobody's hurting you?

105Then it's a sickness from Zeus, and nobody can help with that.

Pray to your father Poseidon. And keep it down.

Their footsteps go away. Silence. Polyphemus feels his way to the door, rolls the stone aside, and sits in the doorway with his arms spread wide. Very quietly, the Crew start the beat.

CREW

whispering, on the beat

Who's in your cave? Nobody.

Who took your eye? Nobody.

Who's going to help you? Nobody.

110Nobody. Nobody. Nobody.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) And my heart laughed, to see how my name had fooled them.

But he sat in the door with his arms out wide,

Feeling for any man sneaking outside.

His rams: thick fleece, wool dark as violet.

115Three tied together, a man under it.

Three rams, one man, till the crew was tied.

The best ram's mine. I clung to his hide.

Hands in the wool, my face to the sky,

We waited for dawn. Then the rams went by.

Beat out

The Crew crawl past Polyphemus, one at a time, low. His hands pass over their backs, not quite touching. Last comes Odysseus, slowly.

POLYPHEMUS

His hands stop over Odysseus.

120Ram. My good ram.

You, first out every morning. First to the grass. First to the river.

Why are you last today?

You are sad for my eye.

A bad man did it. Nobody. He filled my head with wine.

125If you could talk. If you could tell me where he hides.

I would break him on this floor, and my heart would hurt less.

(He lets go.) Go on. Go eat.

ODYSSEUS

And he let that ram walk, with me underneath.

THE MUSE

They ran for the ship. They rowed.

130Here the story could have ended, with a few more strokes.

But he was not yet so far that a man's shout could not be heard.

ODYSSEUS

(shouting) CYCLOPS! That was no weak man's crew you ate!

You ate your guests in your own house! Zeus paid you back!

Polyphemus tears the top off a hill and throws it. Everyone on the ship goes down with the wave.

CREW

Captain, stop! Why poke him?

135He almost sank us! One more word and he'll have our range,

and he'll crush our heads and the ship with one rock!

ODYSSEUS

Beat in: Steps only. Getting louder.

They begged me, every man at his oar:

Captain, enough. Don't say any more.

But Nobody's trick gets Nobody's fame.

140So I stood up tall, and I gave him my name.

Beat out: Dead stop.

CYCLOPS! If anybody ever asks you who took your eye,

say it was ODYSSEUS!

Sacker of cities! Son of Laertes!

Whose home is ITHACA!

Silence. Polyphemus turns his face to the sound. Then he lifts his hands to the sky.

POLYPHEMUS

145Odysseus.

An old prophet told me that name. I watched for a tall man, a strong man.

And you are a little thing who beat me with wine.

(to the sky) Hear me, Poseidon, Earth-shaker, god of the dark hair, if I am your son.

You have a name now. Odysseus, son of Laertes. Ithaca.

150Let him never get home.

But if he is fated to get home, let him come late.

Let him come broken, every man of his dead,

in a stranger's ship.

And let him find trouble in his house.

THE MUSE

155So he prayed. And the god of the dark hair heard him.

One stomp from everyone. Then the beat comes back, one slow step at a time.

ODYSSEUS

to the court

Twelve ships I had. You found me alone.

You fed me. You asked me the way back home.

Nobody saved them from the cave that day.

Somebody's mouth threw the rest away.

Beat out

Athena School Edition · Book XXIII of the Odyssey · about 8 minutes

The Olive Tree

Parts: Penelope, Odysseus, Eurycleia, Telemachus, The Muse, and anyone keeping the beat.

A rule down the left side means the line goes on the beat. Everything else is spoken. Line numbers are for rehearsal.

Two chairs, far apart, facing each other. Odysseus sits in one, in rags, looking at the floor. Upstage, Penelope is asleep on a third chair.

THE MUSE

Night in the palace at Ithaca. The fight is over.

The suitors lie in a heap by the gates of the court.

Up the stairs comes the old nurse, Eurycleia, laughing,

her knees moving fast for joy, her feet stumbling over each other.

EURYCLEIA

5Wake up, Penelope! Wake up, dear child!

Come see with your own eyes what you have wanted every day!

Odysseus is home! He's in this house!

And he has killed the suitors, every one!

PENELOPE

Nurse. The gods have driven you crazy. They can do that.

10Why mock me? That was the best sleep I've had

since the day he sailed for that evil city I won't name.

Any other woman in this house, I'd send her down in tears.

Your age saves you. Go.

EURYCLEIA

I'm not mocking you, child! He's the beggar!

15The stranger they all insulted in the hall!

Telemachus knew, and he kept it quiet.

Penelope jumps up and throws her arms around the old woman.

PENELOPE

Tell me the truth, then. If he's home, how?

One man, and so many of them, always in a pack?

EURYCLEIA

I didn't see. We were locked in. I only heard the groaning.

20Then your son called me, and there he stood among the dead,

stained like a lion with the blood and dirt of battle.

It would have warmed your heart.

Penelope lets go. She steps back. She goes cold.

PENELOPE

Don't gloat yet, nurse.

You know how welcome he would be. To everyone. To me.

25But this isn't true. Some god killed them, angry at their insults.

Odysseus is lost, far away.

EURYCLEIA

Your heart never believes anything!

Then here's your proof: the scar the boar gave him with its tusk.

I saw it when I washed his feet. I tried to tell you,

30and he put his hand over my mouth.

Come and see. If I'm lying, kill me.

PENELOPE

Even you can't read the gods, nurse. But let's go down.

I'll look at the dead, and at the man who killed them.

She walks the whole length of the floor and sits in the far chair, across from him. She looks. He looks at the floor. Nobody speaks. Hold it for a slow count of fifteen. It should feel too long.

THE MUSE

She sat long in silence.

35Now she looked at him, and knew him.

Now she looked at his rags, and she did not.

TELEMACHUS

Mother. Some mother you are.

Why are you sitting over there? Go to him! Ask him something!

No other woman would keep this far from a husband

40home in the twentieth year.

Your heart is harder than stone.

PENELOPE

Beat in: Just Penelope's finger, tapping the arm of her chair. One, two, three, four. No one else.

My son, I can't speak. I can't look at his face.

Twenty years of strangers have come to this place,

Every one with a story, and the story's the same:

45"I saw him. He's coming. I know him by name."

I've fed them. I've cried. I've been played for a fool.

The door stays shut, and I make the rule.

If this is Odysseus, then he and I

Have signs no one knows. So let him try.

Beat out

ODYSSEUS

50(He smiles.) Telemachus, let your mother test me. She'll know soon enough.

Right now I'm filthy and I'm in rags. That's why she won't say it's me.

THE MUSE

They took him to the bath. They put a clean cloak on him,

and Athena poured grace over his head and shoulders,

as a master lays gold over silver.

55He came back looking like a god, and sat down again across from his wife.

She did not move.

ODYSSEUS

(The smile is gone.) Strange woman.

The gods gave you a heart nobody can soften.

No other wife alive would keep away from a husband,

60after all that, home in the twentieth year.

Nurse. Make me up a bed. I'll sleep alone.

Her heart is iron.

Penelope stands. This is the test. She says it lightly, and she does not look at him.

PENELOPE

Strange man.

I'm not proud. I'm not scornful. I'm not amazed.

65I remember very well what you looked like, sailing out of Ithaca.

Eurycleia, make up his bed.

Take the big bed out of our room, the one he built himself.

Move it out here. Put fleeces on it, and blankets.

THE MUSE

So she spoke, and tested her husband.

70The test is hers. She decides when this reunion happens, not him.

ODYSSEUS

(He is on his feet.) WHAT did you say?

Who moved my bed?

That bed can't be moved. Not by any man alive, however strong.

Only if a god came down.

75Because I made that bed. I made it, and no one else.

Beat in: He stamps it himself. Then the others take it up, one by one. Hammer and adze.

That bed's my own design,

An olive tree its spine.

An olive grew inside our wall,

Long in the leaf, like a pillar, tall.

80I didn't cut it. I laid the stone,

I built our room around it, alone.

Then I cut the branches, trimmed the crown,

Left the trunk with its root still driven down.

Smoothed it, trued it, drilled it, and there:

85That's our bedpost. It goes nowhere.

Gold and silver and ivory laid,

Purple oxhide, tight. I made

That bed with these hands. That was my sign.

So tell me, woman: is that bed still mine?

90Or did some man come with a saw,

Cut through that trunk, and move it all?

Beat out: Dead stop.

Silence. Penelope's knees give way. Then she runs, the whole length of the floor, and puts her arms around his neck.

PENELOPE

Don't be angry with me, Odysseus.

You were always the wisest of men.

It was the gods who gave us sorrow.

95They could not bear to let us stay together and be young,

and come to the door of old age side by side.

Don't be angry that I didn't run to you when I first saw you.

All this time my heart has been shaking,

afraid some man would come and fool me with his words.

100There are so many.

But you have told me the sign of our bed.

No one has ever seen it but you, and me, and one maid,

Actor's daughter, who kept our door.

There. You have won it, my stubborn heart.

Odysseus weeps. They hold on.

THE MUSE

105As when the sight of land is welcome to swimmers,

whose ship Poseidon has smashed on the open sea,

and only a few escape the grey water and swim for shore,

their bodies crusted with salt, glad to set foot on land:

so welcome to her was the sight of her husband,

110and her white arms she would not let go from his neck.

And rosy-fingered Dawn would have found them weeping still,

but grey-eyed Athena held the night back at the edge of the west,

and would not let the morning harness her horses.
