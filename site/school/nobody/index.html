Athena School EditionBook IX of the Odyssey

Nobody

Odysseus beats the Cyclops by giving a false name. Then he gives the real one.

Time
About 10 minutes
Cast
3 speakers, plus a small crew
You need
A bare floor and a few chairs. No recording.

All three scripts, ready to print

What happens

Odysseus tells a king's court how he got trapped in a giant's cave. The giant, Polyphemus, is eating his men two at a time. Odysseus gets him drunk, says his name is Nobody, and blinds him with a burning stake. When the giant howls for help, he can only say that Nobody is hurting him, so no one comes. The men escape under the sheep. Then, safe on his ship, Odysseus cannot stand to stay Nobody. He shouts his real name, and the giant prays to his father, Poseidon, to destroy him.

BeatOne or more students keep time with feet and hands. No lines.

Other names: Alcinous (al-SIN-oh-us), Cicones (sih-KOH-neez), Maron (MAY-ron), Laertes (lay-UR-teez), Agamemnon (ag-uh-MEM-non), Poseidon (puh-SY-dun), Ithaca (ITH-uh-kuh). Any student can play any part.

Content note

  • Violence: A giant kills and eats six men. The script says so in blunt words, but the staging shows only actors stepping away and turning their backs. Odysseus and his crew blind the giant with a burning stake. This is mimed, with a scream, and no one touches the actor.
  • Alcohol: Odysseus gets the giant drunk on strong wine as a trick. The bowl is mimed and nothing is drunk on stage.
  • Homer's text: The matching passage is much more graphic than this script. Read it before you hand it out.

The beat

Step, clap, step, clap, slow and heavy, like a giant walking. It drops to a whisper when the men plan, and it stops dead at the moment Odysseus shouts his name.

Count 1, 2, 3, 4. Step on 1. Clap on 2. Step on 3. Clap on 4. Start slow, with a clap about every second and a half. For more drive, add a quick second step right after 3.

Students can also drum on a desk, beatbox, or bring a beat they made themselves. Ask everyone to speak in their own voice. No one needs an accent or a “rap voice.”

No scene here needs a recording. The full Athena was built around popular songs, but this edition uses none of their titles, hooks or lyrics. Use a beat your students make.

If you want to use a real recording: staging one in a show likely needs dramatic rights from the song’s owners, and a school’s usual music license often doesn’t cover that. Check with your school first. This is general information, not legal advice.

Athena School Edition · Book IX of the Odyssey · about 10 minutes

Nobody

Parts: Odysseus, Polyphemus, The Muse, Crew, Cyclopes, and anyone keeping the beat.

A rule down the left side means the line goes on the beat. Everything else is spoken. Line numbers are for rehearsal.

A bare floor. One chair upstage is the cave door. The Crew sit in a huddle. The audience is the court of King Alcinous.

THE MUSE

Hear now of the man of many devices.

Ten years gone from Troy. Wrecked, washed up, and fed

in the hall of King Alcinous, among people who do not know his name.

They feed a stranger first. Then they ask.

5You in the seats: you are that court.

The king has asked. Listen.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) My name? I'll tell you. Remember that I told you.

I am Odysseus, son of Laertes.

My home is Ithaca, a rough rock low in the sea,

10and to me there is nothing sweeter.

Here is what it cost me to say so.

Beat inStep, clap, step, clap. Slow and heavy.

Came out of Troy with twelve black ships,

Salt on my teeth and the war on my lips.

 

Then the Cyclops' land: no law, no plow,

15No court, no council. I'm curious now.

I took twelve men and a goatskin of wine,

Dark, sweet, strong, and the gift was mine:

Maron gave it, Apollo's priest.

One cup in twenty of water: still a feast.

 

20We found his cave, and nobody home.

Cheese in the baskets. Lambs. Alone.

CREW

Captain! Take the cheese! Take the lambs! Let's go!

ODYSSEUS

I looked at my crew and I told them no.

I wanted the giant. I wanted his gift,

25The gift that's owed when you're cast adrift.

Beat out

(to the court) It would have been far better to take the cheese.

THE MUSE

Then he came home, that monstrous thing,

like a wooded peak of the high hills, standing apart and alone.

He threw down his firewood, and the cave shook.

30He lifted a doorstone

that twenty-two wagons could not have moved,

and he set it in the door.

Polyphemus fills the space. The Crew shrink back.

POLYPHEMUS

RUMBLE. ROAR.

This cave mine. These sheep mine.

He milks. He lights the fire. He sees them.

35Strangers. Who are you?

Traders? Or pirates, robbing the salt roads?

ODYSSEUS

We are Greeks, lord, blown off course from Troy.

We come to your knees. Give us what a stranger is owed.

Fear the gods. Zeus himself looks after strangers.

POLYPHEMUS

40You stupid, little man. Or you come from far.

Cyclopes do not fear Zeus. We are better than Zeus.

I spare you if I want. Not for Zeus.

Where is your ship? Far? Near? Tell me.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) He thought he could catch me. Me.

45(to Polyphemus) What ship? Poseidon broke it on your rocks.

We are all that's left.

Polyphemus says nothing. He reaches into the Crew. Two of them step back into the dark and turn away. That is all we see.

POLYPHEMUS

CRUNCH. CRUNCH.

Men between teeth. No mercy in hunger.

He lies down among his sheep and sleeps.

ODYSSEUS

I put my hand on my sword. One push, under the ribs.

50And then? Who moves the stone? Twenty-two wagons couldn't.

We'd die in the dark, behind a dead giant's door.

So I took my hand away, and we waited for the dawn.

THE MUSE

Early Dawn shone forth, the rosy-fingered.

He ate two more.

55Then he rolled away the stone, drove his flocks out,

and set it back as lightly as you'd put the lid on a quiver.

ODYSSEUS

Beat inFaster and quieter. The Crew whisper it with him.

By the pen lay his club, green olive wood,

Long as a ship's mast. There it stood.

Cut off six feet. Crew, make it clean.

60I shaved the point, burned it hard and mean.

Hid it in the dung where the sheep had been.

Cast lots: who lifts it? Four men in,

The same four men I would have picked.

I make five. Now here's the trick.

Beat out

THE MUSE

65Evening. He came home. He set the stone. He milked. He ate two more.

That was six.

And Odysseus stood up, with a bowl of ivy wood in his hands.

ODYSSEUS

Cyclops. Here. Drink. You've had your meal of men.

I brought this as an offering, hoping you'd pity me and send me home.

70But you're out of your mind. Who will ever visit you again?

Polyphemus takes the bowl and drinks. Mime it. The bowl can be empty hands.

POLYPHEMUS

Good. GOOD.

This is nectar.

Again. And your name, little man. Tell me.

I give you a stranger's gift. You'll be glad.

THE MUSE

75Three times he poured. Three times, in his folly, the giant drank it down.

ODYSSEUS

You ask my famous name, Cyclops? I'll tell you.

Then give me the gift you promised.

Nobody. My name is Nobody.

My mother, my father, all my friends call me Nobody.

POLYPHEMUS

80(slowly, very pleased) Nobody.

Nobody I eat last. All your friends first.

Nobody last.

That is your gift.

He falls back. He sleeps.

ODYSSEUS

Beat inOne finger on a table. Then two. Then the Crew.

Shove that stake down deep in the ash,

85Green wood glowing. Now move. Fast.

Five of us lift it, a god in our chests.

I lean from above and the crew does the rest.

Turn it like a drill through a ship's thick beam.

Hear it hiss. Now hear him scream.

Beat out

All of this is mime. The stake is made of air. No one touches Polyphemus.

POLYPHEMUS

90AAAAH!

CYCLOPES! NEIGHBORS! HELP ME!

CYCLOPES

(from far off, half asleep) Polyphemus! It's the middle of the night!

What is wrong with you?

Is somebody stealing your sheep? Is somebody killing you?

POLYPHEMUS

95FRIENDS! NOBODY IS KILLING ME!

NOBODY! BY A TRICK!

CYCLOPES

Nobody? Nobody's hurting you?

Then it's a sickness from Zeus, and nobody can help with that.

Pray to your father Poseidon. And keep it down.

Their footsteps go away. Silence. Polyphemus feels his way to the door, rolls the stone aside, and sits in the doorway with his arms spread wide. Very quietly, the Crew start the beat.

CREW

whispering, on the beat

100Who's in your cave? Nobody.

Who took your eye? Nobody.

Who's going to help you? Nobody.

Nobody. Nobody. Nobody.

ODYSSEUS

(to the court) And my heart laughed, to see how my name had fooled them.

105His rams: thick fleece, wool dark as violet.

Three tied together, a man under it.

Three rams, one man, till the crew was tied.

The best ram's mine. I clung to his hide.

Hands in the wool, my face to the sky,

110We waited for dawn. Then the rams went by.

Beat out

The Crew crawl past Polyphemus, one at a time, low. His hands pass over their backs, not quite touching. Last comes Odysseus, slowly.

POLYPHEMUS

His hands stop over Odysseus.

Ram. My good ram.

You, first out every morning. First to the grass. First to the river.

Why are you last today?

You are sad for my eye.

115A bad man did it. Nobody. He filled my head with wine.

If you could talk. If you could tell me where he hides.

I would break him on this floor, and my heart would hurt less.

(He lets go.) Go on. Go eat.

ODYSSEUS

And he let that ram walk, with me underneath.

THE MUSE

120They ran for the ship. They rowed.

Here the story could have ended, with a few more strokes.

But he was not yet so far that a man's shout could not be heard.

ODYSSEUS

(shouting) CYCLOPS! That was no weak man's crew you ate!

You ate your guests in your own house! Zeus paid you back!

Polyphemus tears the top off a hill and throws it. Everyone on the ship goes down with the wave.

CREW

125Captain, stop! He almost sank us!

One more word and he'll crush our heads and the ship with one rock!

ODYSSEUS

Beat inSteps only. Getting louder.

They begged me, every man at his oar:

Captain, enough. Don't say any more.

But Nobody's trick gets Nobody's fame.

130So I stood up tall, and I gave him my name.

Beat outDead stop.

CYCLOPS! If anybody ever asks you who took your eye,

say it was ODYSSEUS!

Sacker of cities! Son of Laertes!

Whose home is ITHACA!

Silence. Polyphemus turns his face to the sound. Then he lifts his hands to the sky.

POLYPHEMUS

135Odysseus.

(to the sky) Hear me, Poseidon, Earth-shaker, god of the dark hair, if I am your son.

You have a name now. Odysseus, son of Laertes. Ithaca.

Let him never get home.

But if he is fated to get home, let him come late.

140Let him come broken, every man of his dead,

in a stranger's ship.

And let him find trouble in his house.

THE MUSE

So he prayed. And the god of the dark hair heard him.

One stomp from everyone. Then the beat comes back, one slow step at a time.

ODYSSEUS

to the court

Twelve ships I had. You found me alone.

145You fed me. You asked me the way back home.

Nobody saved them from the cave that day.

Somebody's mouth threw the rest away.

Beat out

Three prompts

  1. Rehearse. Play the shouted name three ways: as triumph, as rage, and as a man who cannot help himself. Have the Crew beg louder each time. Which version makes the audience feel the mistake?
  2. Discuss. Who is the monster here? Polyphemus eats six men, and he also speaks gently to his ram. Odysseus saves his crew, and then his shout helps get them killed. Point to the lines that make your case.
  3. Discuss. Why does Odysseus need to be Nobody in order to survive, and why can't he stay Nobody? When have you wanted credit badly enough to pay for it?

The matching passage of Homer

Read Book IX, from the land of the Cyclopes to the second rock (https://athena.arc-codex.com/school/homer/book-09.html), in Butcher and Lang’s public-domain translation.

Where this scene differs from Homer

  • Three days in the cave pass in a few minutes. Six men still die, as in Homer, but we hear about it and do not see it.
  • We cut the Cicones, the lotus-eaters, the island of goats, the prophecy Polyphemus remembers, and the feast after the escape.
  • The closing lines of regret are ours. Homer's Odysseus says only that it would have been better to leave the cave. He never says he regrets the shout, though his crew begs him not to.
